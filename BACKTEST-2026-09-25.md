# Backtest: "copy the leaderboard whales" (run 2026-09-25, script `scripts/backtest-whales.ts`)

**Method.** For each leaderboard trader: pull up to 1,500 most recent trades from `data-api.polymarket.com/trades`, group by market+outcome, resolve every market through `clob.polymarket.com/markets/<conditionId>` (`tokens[].winner`), and compute the P/L of copying every BUY at the whale's own fill price and holding to resolution (`copyPnl`, `copyROI`). `whaleNet` is the whale's own realized P/L on the same resolved markets (sells counted). 5-minute/15-minute crypto up-or-down scalps excluded. 8,992 markets resolved.

**Cohorts.** A = top 15 by 30-day P/L (selected on the outcome measured → survivorship-biased). B = top 15 by all-time volume (selected on activity, not outcome).

| Cohort | traders | resolved positions | copy win-rate | copy staked | copy P/L | **copy ROI** | whales' own net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: top 30d P/L | 15 | 6,142 | 49.9 % | $74.0M | +$1.23M | **+1.7 %** | +$1.86M |
| B: top all-time volume | 15 | 6,880 | 54.7 % | $53.9M | −$0.43M | **−0.8 %** | −$0.08M |

Per-trader spread inside cohort A: −8.8 % (maz26) to +41 % (Herdonia, 106 resolved, 100 % sports). Sports are 50–100 % of every whale's book.

**Reading.**
1. Blind copying of "top" wallets has no edge: +1.7 % in the biased cohort, −0.8 % in the unbiased one, before your own slippage and the price impact of copying.
2. The leaderboard rewards volume in sports handicaps and totals, where whales are ~50/50 pickers on large size. Their P/L is variance, not calibration.
3. The spread between wallets (−12 % to +41 %) is where a product can live: score by calibrated ROI **per category** with significance tests, weight recent windows, and only follow above-normal size from wallets that pass. That is Phase 2 (`lib/intel/wallet-score.ts`).
4. Never compute win rates from `/positions`: winners get redeemed and vanish, losers stay at $0. A first draft of this backtest did that and reported 0 % win rates.

**Caveats.** No leaderboard history exists, so cohort A is in-sample by construction. Copy fills assume the whale's price with zero slippage. Trades capped at 1,500 per wallet (newest first), so heavy traders are measured on their last ~2 weeks.

# Round 2 (afternoon): walk-forward test of the scoring thesis · `scripts/backtest-walkforward.ts`

**Question.** Does calibrated-ROI + significance selection, computed only on data known before a cutoff, predict copy returns after it?

**Method.** In-sample: positions from trades before the cutoff on markets that had *ended* before the cutoff (no lookahead on resolutions). Sharp = ≥30 resolved, calibrated ROI > 0, one-sided p < 0.1. Fade = significant losers. Control = other wallets with ≥30 resolved. Out-of-sample: every BUY after the cutoff copied at fill + 50 bps, held to resolution. Two ROI views: dollar-weighted (whale sizes dominate) and equal-weight ($1 per position, what a copier with fixed size gets).

| cutoff | group | positions | win rate | $-weighted ROI | equal-weight ROI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 d | sharp (14 wallets) | 887 | 56 % | −4.2 % | **+0.3 %** |
| 7 d | fade (2) | 46 | 63 % | +43.7 % | +5.1 % |
| 7 d | control (39) | 2,171 | 55 % | +7.2 % | +15.2 % |
| 14 d | sharp (14) | 1,443 | 58 % | +6.4 % | **−0.7 %** |
| 14 d | fade (2) | 43 | 70 % | +38.6 % | +40.6 % |
| 14 d | control (35) | 3,415 | 54 % | +5.1 % | +10.3 % |

Per wallet (7 d): 11 of 14 sharp wallets were positive; one (BrotherObama, −$729k on $1.5M) wiped the dollar-weighted aggregate. 99.9 % of sharp out-of-sample positions were sports.

**Verdict.** No predictive edge from the current scoring at this sample size: sharp ≈ 0 % equal-weight, control better, fade candidates kept winning. This is shown as-is on `/intel/backtest`. What would change it: wallet coverage (63 → 500+ wallets with history), longer history (≥ 90 days; the Data API pages back further than 3,000 trades), non-sports filtering at selection time, and requiring p < 0.01. Until then the wallet tables are descriptive, not a trading signal.

**Other round-2 checks.** 40/40 randomly sampled stored resolutions agree with Gamma's post-close prices. Three wallets recomputed from raw trades match the stored scores exactly (`scripts/audit-recompute.ts`); a fourth (ndb1) differed only because scores predated 33k newer resolutions — rescored. Insider "sniper" clusters were 119 of 134 flags and were ordinary Fed-market trading; the rule now needs a material cluster (≥2 % of 24 h volume or a thin market) with ≥2 young wallets. Strategy backtests on 28 non-sports markets with stored prices: near-certainty 0 trades, deadline-decay 0 trades, thin-move −2.7 %.
